## Reference and reproduced results

The bundled result files are the evidence generated during preparation of the
camera-ready paper. They are the source for exact numerical comparison.

A fresh execution writes new model- and platform-dependent outputs. Reviewers
should not overwrite or reinterpret the bundled values as if they were created
by the fresh run. Autoencoder runs use distinct run-tag directories under
`results/autoencoder_multiseed/`.

Large supervised serialized models are not distributed because they total
approximately 1.3 GB and include a Random Forest near 784 MB. Workflow 1
regenerates them before the operational and XAI workflows.

## Cross-platform interpretation

The bundled five-seed autoencoder reference was executed on Windows CPU. The
provided Docker image executes TensorFlow on Linux CPU. Fixed application-level
seeds do not make TensorFlow optimization bit-identical across operating
systems, library builds, oneDNN kernels, thread schedules, or floating-point
operation orders.

Consequently, Docker metrics may be slightly lower than the paper values and an
individual seed may converge to a different local optimum. This is acceptable
only when the run:

1. uses matching dataset, split, feature, and class fingerprints;
2. completes every requested seed;
3. produces the documented outputs;
4. preserves the principal qualitative conclusion; and
5. reports the observed variation instead of silently replacing the reference
   values.

For the autoencoder, the paper reference at P85 is attack recall
`0.994 +/- 0.006`, benign FPR `0.244 +/- 0.003`, and AUROC
`0.878 +/- 0.036`. These values are comparison anchors, not bit-exact pass
conditions for a Linux Docker rerun. Threshold monotonicity and explicit FPR
reporting remain required.

Operational latency and throughput are even more hardware-sensitive. Table 10
records the reference workstation measurements; a reviewer should verify that
the benchmark executes and interpret absolute values in the context of the
review machine.

## Scope notes

- Autoencoder rows that may appear in classifier-style supporting CSVs are not
  19-class paper claims. The paper evaluates the autoencoder as a binary
  benign-versus-attack anomaly screen.
- SHAP and LIME outputs are attribution sanity checks, not causal explanations.
- The paper's supervised bootstrap intervals describe test-set resampling of a
  fixed trained model; the autoencoder intervals describe variability across
  five training runs. These must not be conflated.
- Source CICIoMT2024 records are not distributed by this artifact.
